The fate line is unusual among the major lines in how much its starting point varies from hand to hand — and in how much interpretive weight the classical tradition gives to that variation. Two fate lines can be equally deep, equally straight, and equally long, and still be read differently because of where they begin.

This article assumes you have already located a fate line on your palm and want to go further on one specific question: where does it start, and what does that starting point traditionally mean? The [main fate line guide](/blog/beginner/fate-line/) covers the line more broadly — its overall associations, its path, its ending point, and what to make of an absent line. If you are not sure whether you have a fate line at all, the dedicated [no fate line article](/blog/beginner/no-fate-line-meaning/) is the better starting point.

## Locating the starting point

Hold your active hand palm-up and find the fate line: a roughly vertical line running up the centre of the palm toward the base of the middle finger, where it points at the Mount of Saturn. Rather than tracing the line upward, look instead at its lowest visible point — the place where it first appears. That point is the starting point, and it can fall in several different regions of the lower palm.

Not every hand has a fate line, and a significant number of people have none at all. That is its own reading and is covered in the article linked above — this piece is only for hands where a fate line, or a section of one, is visible.

## Why the starting point matters

The starting point is read as an indication of when and how a sense of direction establishes itself in the reading, not as a prediction of what that direction will be. A line beginning low on the palm is associated with an early sense of purpose; a line beginning higher up is associated with direction that takes longer to settle. Neither is read as better than the other, and the tradition treats both as normal variation rather than as a sign of anything gone right or wrong.

## The four starting points

### From the wrist or the base of the palm

When the fate line rises from low on the palm, at or near the wrist, it is traditionally associated with a sense of direction or vocation that established itself early in life. Cheiro read a wrist-originating fate line as belonging to someone whose path is long and continuous — a consistent thread that runs from youth onward rather than one that is picked up partway through life.

### From the Mount of Luna

The Mount of Luna sits at the outer edge of the lower palm, on the side opposite the thumb. A fate line rising from Luna has several distinct readings attached to it in the sources, and they should not be collapsed into one statement.

Benham's own formulation is specific and historical: when the fate line "rises from the Mount of Moon and runs on to the Mount of Saturn the subject's success in life will be materially assisted by one of the opposite sex." That is Benham's exact claim, tied to gender, and it should be presented as his historical reading rather than as a general truth. Separately, Cheiro read a Luna-rooted fate line as one whose course is more changeable, shaped by the approval or response of people outside the person's own control. West, writing later, extends the association further still, describing a Luna-rooted fate line as particularly common among people whose path involves the public, collaboration, or work that depends on an audience.

These are three related but distinct observations — Benham's historical claim about assistance from someone of the opposite sex, Cheiro's claim about a course shaped by outside approval, and West's broader extension toward public or collaborative life — rather than three ways of saying the same thing. Treat the later, broader readings as extensions of Benham's original point, not as his own words.

### From the life line

When the fate line separates from the life line and rises independently, the association shifts toward a direction built through personal effort rather than one given at the outset. Gettings reads this configuration as a path the person constructs themselves — one that shares its source with the life line's own vitality before breaking away to establish its own course. Cheiro reads it similarly, as a self-made direction that emerges from the conditions of a person's own life rather than from external structure or inheritance.

### Starting higher — mid-palm or later

When no fate line is visible in the lower palm and the line only appears from roughly the middle of the palm upward, often near or after the head line, the association is with a direction that clarifies or consolidates during the middle part of life rather than early on. West and Fincham both note that a late-starting fate line is common among people who find their vocation, or settle on their path, through accumulated experience rather than through early clarity. The tradition is explicit that this is not a deficiency — a direction reached later is still a direction.

## Reading the starting point in context

A starting point is one observation among several, and the fate line lesson makes the point directly: a faint fate line rising from Luna on a hand with a strong Apollo mount and a clear head line tells a different story than the same faint line on a hand where the other markers are also indistinct. The starting point tells you something about when and how direction establishes itself; the line's depth, path, and endpoint, along with the rest of the hand, tell you the rest. None of these features is read as though it stood alone, and no starting point should be combined with another feature — a mount, another line, a break — to produce a specific new meaning unless that exact combination is already established in the tradition.

## Common myths

**"A fate line from Luna means your success depends entirely on someone else."** This overstates and flattens three separate, narrower readings into one deterministic claim. Benham's own formulation is specific to assistance from someone of the opposite sex; Cheiro's is about a course shaped by outside approval; West's later extension is about public or collaborative work. None of them describes dependency, and none removes the person's own agency from the reading.

**"A late-starting fate line means you wasted your early years."** The classical sources do not treat a late start as lost time. A direction that consolidates in the middle of life is read as a legitimate and common pattern, not as a delay to be corrected or regretted.

**"A fate line starting at the wrist means your path was decided before you were born."** An early starting point is associated with an early sense of direction, not with a predetermined destiny. The tradition this site draws from — Cheiro, Benham, Gettings, West, and Fincham among them — reads the fate line for tendencies, not for a fixed script.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where does the fate line usually start?**
There is no single typical starting point. The classical sources describe several — the wrist or base of the palm, the Mount of Luna, the life line, and a start that comes later in the mid-palm — and each is a normal variation with its own association, not a deviation from a standard.

**What does it mean if my fate line starts at the wrist?**
It is traditionally associated with a sense of direction or vocation that established itself early in life, and with a path read as a long, continuous thread rather than one adopted partway through life.

**What does it mean if my fate line starts from the Mount of Luna?**
It depends on which reading you are drawing from. Benham's own claim ties it to assistance from someone of the opposite sex; Cheiro reads it as a course shaped by outside approval; later writers such as West read it more broadly as common among people whose path involves the public or sustained collaboration with others.

**What does it mean if my fate line starts from the life line?**
It is associated with a self-made direction — one built through personal effort and circumstance, sharing its origin with the life line before establishing its own separate course.

**What if my fate line doesn't start until partway up my palm?**
A fate line that begins in the middle of the palm, rather than lower down, is associated with a direction that clarifies or consolidates during the middle period of life, often after a period of varied experience. This is a common pattern and is not read as a deficiency.
