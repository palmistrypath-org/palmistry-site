# PP-RELAY-036 Evidence — Benham on the Via Lascivia

## Source
William G. Benham, *The Laws of Scientific Hand Reading: A Practical Treatise on the Art Commonly Called Palmistry* (G. P. Putnam, 1900/1901), Part Second, Chapter XIV, “The Minor Lines,” section “The Via Lascivia.” Public-domain edition metadata is independently corroborated by Google Books/Open Library.

Director verification sources used on 2026-08-21:
- Google Books full-view metadata for the 1900 G. P. Putnam edition.
- Open Library record for the 1901 edition.
- Two online transcriptions reproducing Benham’s Via Lascivia section (Chest of Books / Superphysics) were compared for the specific claims below.

## Verified safe claim boundary
Benham:
- recognizes a named line called the Via Lascivia;
- treats it as a sister line to the Line of Mercury in the dedicated section, while elsewhere saying he regards it more as a chance line than a fixed minor line;
- describes its usual/slanting position as rising from the inside of the base of the Mount of Moon and ending on the lower part of the upper Mount of Moon near the percussion;
- says its historical name arose from an older reading that linked a strong Mercury line plus a sister line with surplus vitality/energy and, in a sensual hand type, lascivious behavior;
- explicitly qualifies that reading by saying a non-sensual hand could express the same surplus energy in other activity instead.

## Important exclusions
Do **not** use Benham to support any of the following as fact or Palmistry Path guidance:
- medical diagnosis, disease, digestion, liver, stomach, health, vitality, allergy, sensitivity, intolerance, or any other health claim;
- criminality, rape, murder, arson, moral character, dangerousness, or other harmful/deterministic claims found in Benham’s period text;
- modern prevalence or rarity claims (including repeating Benham’s “seldom seen” wording as a current prevalence fact);
- modern practitioner consensus;
- empirical/scientific validation;
- a claim that “Via Lascivia” and “allergy line” are historically equivalent terms. Benham evidence reviewed here does not use or establish the “allergy line” label.

## Editorial use for PP-RELAY-036
A source-safe article may:
- explain where Benham places/describes the line;
- explain that the historical name reflects period palmistry ideas about appetite/energy/excess, with careful attribution and without endorsing those ideas as fact;
- explicitly distinguish Palmistry Path’s non-medical framing from historical health/excess claims;
- address “allergy line” as a modern search term/question only by saying the reviewed classical evidence does not establish that label or a medical allergy meaning, unless separate already-approved repository evidence proves otherwise;
- advise readers not to use palm lines to diagnose allergies or health conditions.
