# Worksheet Pack — Product Specification

*Internal planning document. Not for publication. Last updated: 2026-05-27.*

---

## 1. Overview and Purpose

The Palmistry Path Worksheet Pack is a printable PDF collection designed for self-study practice. It is aimed at beginners and early intermediate learners who have read some of the site's articles or worked through the foundations curriculum and want a structured way to apply what they have learned to a real hand — their own or a willing subject's.

The pack is not a reference card or a summary of meanings. It is a practice tool: guided observation sheets that prompt the learner to look carefully, describe accurately, and hold their interpretations lightly. That framing is consistent with the site's core principle — "practice over prediction, observation over memorisation" — and positions the product as an honest educational resource rather than a fortune-telling shortcut.

The worksheet format suits this audience because:
- Reading about a palm line is not the same as finding it on a real hand. Worksheets force the gap between description and observation.
- Beginners benefit from guided structure. Open-ended prompts ("describe your heart line") are more useful than multiple-choice options, which would falsely imply singular correct answers.
- Printables fit the learner's existing session: a beginner reading the site is typically also holding their hand up to the light. A downloadable PDF completes that moment without requiring an account or screen-switching mid-practice.

The product aligns with the build philosophy: content first, products only once the content base earns them.

**Format:** Print-and-write. Not fillable PDF (fillable PDFs require more authoring overhead and behave inconsistently across PDF readers; version 1 should be as frictionless as possible to produce and distribute).

**Paper size:** A4 and US Letter layouts, or a single layout that fits both (leaving sufficient margins). The design should be tested at both sizes before release.

**Estimated page count:** 12–16 pages total across all 7 worksheets. Some worksheets are single pages; the two-hand comparison sheet and the practice reading template will be longer.

## 3. Worksheet Specifications

### Worksheet 1 — Hand Observation Sheet

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
The first step in any reading is systematic observation, not interpretation. This sheet trains the learner to look before they conclude. It covers hand type, texture, flexibility, nail shape, and overall proportions before a single line is mapped. The goal is to develop the habit of description-first practice.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Date and session notes (brief header, 2 lines)
- Hand outline box: large empty rectangle or hand silhouette for tracing or rough sketch
- Hand type: tick boxes for Earth / Air / Fire / Water, with one-line prompt: "What led you to this classification?"
- Texture and feel: short free-text ("firm / soft / mixed — describe")
- Flexibility note: "Does the hand bend back easily?" (yes / no / partially)
- Finger length relative to palm: short / long / proportionate
- Dominant features (free-text, 3–4 lines): "Before looking at lines — what stands out most about this hand?"

**Design notes:**
- Hand outline box should take up roughly a third of the first page — generous enough for a rough trace or sketch
- Two-column layout below the hand box: left column for observations, right column for initial impressions
- No meaning prompts on this sheet — deliberately. The observation sheet is for recording, not interpreting.

**Supporting articles:**
- /blog/beginner/how-to-read-a-palm/
- /blog/beginner/hand-shapes/
- /blog/beginner/what-palmistry-is/ (grounding context)
- /blog/beginner/palmistry-chart-for-beginners/
- /blog/beginner/nails-in-palmistry/
- /blog/beginner/printable-palmistry-worksheets/ (hub article, primary reference)

**Gaps to resolve before building:**

### Worksheet 2 — Major Lines Worksheet

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
Maps the four major lines — heart, head, life, fate — giving learners a structured way to locate, trace, and describe each one before consulting meanings. The structure reflects the site's article architecture: one line at a time, in a consistent format.

**Key sections and fields:**
- For each of the four lines (heart, head, life, fate): a mini hand diagram with the line highlighted for reference, plus fields for:
  - Line present? (yes / faint / absent)
  - Start point (described in anatomical terms: "starts under index finger / between index and middle / from the edge of the palm")
  - End point ("ends under ring finger / curves toward index / fades before reaching edge")
  - Quality: "Deep / fine / broken / chained / clear — circle any that apply"
  - Length relative to the palm: short / medium / long
  - Notable features (2 lines free-text)
- Bottom of sheet: "Overall impression of the major lines — what pattern do you notice across all four?" (3–4 lines)

**Design notes:**
- Four equal-size blocks, one per line, each containing its mini diagram and fields
- Mini diagrams should be simple line-art, not detailed illustrations — a palm outline with a single line marked
- The fate line block should include a note: "The fate line is absent on many hands — record 'absent' if not visible"

### Worksheet 3 — Line Variation Checklist

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
Line variations — breaks, chains, forks, islands, branches — are among the most searched topics on the site. Learners who can name a variation but cannot find it consistently on a real hand need a structured checklist to scan for. This sheet turns a common question ("what does a broken heart line look like?") into a guided search task.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Section A: Heart line variations — check boxes for: broken, chained, forked, double (Girdle of Venus present), island(s), straight vs. curved (check one)
- Section B: Head line variations — check boxes for: broken, forked, island(s), sloping steeply / running straight / curving upward (check one)
- Section C: Life line variations — check boxes for: broken, double (second line alongside), island(s), short, chained section
- Section D: Fate line variations — check boxes for: absent, broken, starts from life line / wrist / lunar mount (check one), ends early
- Section E: Special formations — check boxes for: M line (heart + head + life clearly form the letter), simian line (head and heart merged), crosses on the palm, stars on the palm
- Section F: What I found today (3–4 lines free-text, low-stakes observation prompt)

**Design notes:**
- Checklist format throughout — the only worksheet in the pack that is mostly tick boxes
- Each line variation should have a 4–6 word plain-language description in brackets: "broken (clear gap across the line)"
- Leave generous white space — this sheet will be used while actively examining a hand; cramped layouts are frustrating in practice
- Two columns: variations on the left, a "notes" margin column on the right

- /blog/beginner/forked-heart-line-meaning/
- /blog/beginner/broken-life-line-meaning/

Dedicated articles are not required before building this worksheet. Commission those articles if traffic data later suggests demand.

### Worksheet 4 — Mounts Worksheet

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
The mounts are one of the most spatially confusing topics for beginners because their names do not correspond to their physical positions in an obvious way. This worksheet builds spatial literacy first: locate each mount, assess its development, then note any associations from memory before checking the article.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Labelled hand diagram: a blank palm outline with mount zones marked by number only (no names) — the learner writes the name beside each number as a recall exercise
- For each of the 8 mounts (Jupiter, Saturn, Apollo, Mercury, Venus, Luna, Inner Mars, Outer Mars):
  - Mount name (filled in from the diagram exercise)
  - Development: flat / average / well-developed / overdeveloped (circle one)
  - Dominant feature of this area (1–2 lines)
- Overall notes: "Which mount is most prominent? Which is least developed?" (2 lines each)
- Cross-tradition note at bottom of sheet: "In Indian Hasta Samudrika Shastra, mount emphasis carries additional associations — refer to the mounts overview for detail."

**Design notes:**
- The labelled hand diagram is the centrepiece — it should be generous, taking up roughly half the first page
- Mars is often confusing because Inner Mars and Outer Mars are distinct zones; the diagram should mark both clearly, and the worksheet should treat them as separate items
- Consider a simple development rating scale (1–3) as an alternative to the text options, as long as the language is clear this is a personal observation, not an objective score

### Worksheet 5 — Hand Shape / Finger / Thumb / Nail Worksheet

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
Covers the qualitative features of the hand that are not lines or mounts: shape classification, finger proportions, finger shapes (knotted / smooth / tapered / spatulate), thumb type, and nail shape. These are often covered quickly in beginner material, but careful observation of these features changes interpretation significantly. The worksheet slows the learner down.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Hand shape: Earth / Air / Fire / Water (check one) with "what tipped the classification?" (2 lines)
- Overall hand proportions: palm longer than fingers / fingers longer than palm / roughly equal (check one)
- Finger shape: for each finger (index, middle, ring, little), record shape: tapered / spatulate / square / knotted or smooth joints (circle)
- Finger lengths: index vs. ring comparison (index longer / ring longer / equal) — note this is a factual observation, not a personality verdict
- Thumb: length (short / medium / long relative to the hand), flexibility (stiff / moderate / flexible), shape of tip (flat / rounded / bulbous / waist)
- Nail shape: broad / narrow / square / almond / fan-shaped (circle one per nail if they differ)
- Free notes: "What combination of features stands out on this hand?" (3–4 lines)

**Design notes:**
- Small finger diagrams showing the four basic shapes (tapered, spatulate, square, knotted) would reduce ambiguity for beginners; these can be simple line drawings
- The index-vs-ring comparison field should carry a neutral label — nothing implying a personality trait follows from it
- Nail shape section can be abbreviated to dominant nail type if the learner does not want to record each finger individually

**Supporting articles:**
- /blog/beginner/hand-shapes/
- /blog/beginner/finger-shapes-palmistry/
- /blog/beginner/thumb-meaning-palmistry/
- /blog/beginner/nails-in-palmistry/

**Gaps to resolve before building:**
- No dedicated articles on phalange zones (lower, middle, upper sections of each finger) or the index-to-ring length ratio. These can be treated as observation prompts on the worksheet without requiring full articles first. If traffic data suggests demand, commission those articles before a second edition of the pack.

### Worksheet 6 — Two-Hand Comparison Sheet

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
The active/passive hand comparison is one of the most practically useful concepts in palmistry — and one of the most frequently misunderstood. This sheet structures a side-by-side comparison of the same features across both hands so the learner can see differences directly rather than trying to remember them.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Header: "Active hand (dominant): _____ / Passive hand (non-dominant): _____" (filled in by learner)
- Comparison table with rows for:
  - Heart line: length, quality, notable variations
  - Head line: length, trajectory, notable variations
  - Life line: length, quality, notable variations
  - Fate line: present / absent, strength
  - Major mounts: which are prominent on each hand?
  - Overall quality of lines (clearer and deeper on which hand?)
- Observations section: "What changes between the two hands?" (4–5 lines)
- Prompt: "In Western tradition, the passive hand is often interpreted as potential or inherited tendency; the active hand as how that has developed. What do you notice through that lens?" (3 lines)

**Design notes:**
- Side-by-side two-column layout for the comparison table: left column active hand, right column passive hand, with row labels in the centre
- Two small hand outline boxes at the top — one for each hand — where the learner can make quick sketch notes
- The interpretive prompt at the bottom should be clearly framed as one tradition's perspective, not a universal rule

**Supporting articles:**
- /blog/beginner/which-hand-to-read-palmistry/
- /blog/beginner/printable-palmistry-worksheets/ (includes a full two-hand comparison section)
- Lesson: /learn/foundations/04-active-and-passive-hand/

**Gaps to resolve before building:**
None. The article and lesson together give comprehensive coverage of the concept.

### Worksheet 7 — Practice Reading Template

**Purpose and pedagogical intent:**
This is the synthesis worksheet. After completing worksheets 1–6 on a given hand, the learner uses this template to write a structured, provisional reading — not a prediction, but an organised description of what they observed and what the traditions suggest about it. The framing throughout emphasises tentativeness and multi-tradition awareness.

**Key sections and fields:**
- Session header: date, whose hand, active hand (left / right)
- Summary of hand type and first impressions (4 lines)
- Major lines summary: one paragraph-length field per line, with a subprompt: "What did you observe? What does the tradition suggest? Where are you uncertain?"
- Mounts: "Which mounts were most prominent? What do the traditions associate with that?" (3–4 lines)
- Hand shape and fingers: brief characterisation (2–3 lines)
- Two-hand comparison notes: "What differences did you notice?" (2–3 lines)
- Cross-tradition check: "Did you notice any features where Western, Indian, or Chinese traditions interpret differently? Note them here." (3 lines)
- Provisional synthesis: "If you were describing this hand to the subject in one paragraph, what would you say — without predictions, without certainties?" (8–10 lines)
- Reflection: "What would you look for differently next time?" (2–3 lines)

**Design notes:**
- This is the longest worksheet — allow 3–4 pages
- Generous line spacing and field sizes: learners writing by hand need room
- A small reminder box at the top: "Describe what you see. Note what the traditions suggest. Hold it lightly." — this keeps the reading grounded and reinforces the brand's editorial position
- No meaning-lookup tables embedded in this sheet; the learner should be working from their own notes and the other worksheets by this point

**Supporting articles:**
- /blog/beginner/how-to-read-a-palm/ (primary reference)
- Lesson: /learn/advanced/04-how-to-give-a-reading/ (curriculum counterpart)

**Gaps to resolve before building:**
None. The advanced lesson `/learn/advanced/04-how-to-give-a-reading/` is confirmed to exist at `src/content/lessons/advanced/04-how-to-give-a-reading.mdx` — safe to cross-reference.

## 4. Free vs Paid Tiers

### Free tier (lead magnet upgrade)

**Worksheet 1 — Hand Observation Sheet** is offered as part of the Starter Guide at `/guide/`. The Starter Guide already promises a "Printable Observation Sheet" as one of its deliverables. Worksheet 1 fulfils that promise directly; it should be the version distributed as the guide download, not a separate one-off design.

**Worksheet 3 — Line Variation Checklist** is a natural standalone freebie or content upgrade. The 11 line variation articles (broken heart line, simian line, M line, etc.) generate search traffic from readers who have found a specific feature and want to know what it means. A checklist that covers all common variations gives those readers a reason to join the email list. Placement: at the end of any line variation article, or as a sidebar opt-in on the major lines articles.

### Paid tier

All 7 worksheets bundled as a single PDF — the complete Palmistry Path Worksheet Pack.

**Suggested price:** $7–$9 USD. Rationale: this is the impulse-buy range for printable PDFs; comparable to a single chapter from a paperback palmistry reference. It is low enough to purchase without deliberation, high enough to signal that the product has real content. Price should be set at $7 for launch, with the option to raise to $9 after the first 50 purchases without adjusting the product.

**Purchase model:** One-time payment, immediate PDF download. No subscription, no account required. The learner owns the file and can print it as many times as they like.

**Note:** No checkout, payment processor, or store implementation is scoped in this document. Distribution platform (Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, or self-hosted) is an implementation decision for a later session.

## 5. What NOT to Include

The following are explicitly forbidden in all worksheet content — fields, prompts, labels, instructions, and any embedded text:

- **Predictions or fate claims.** Never prompt the learner to draw conclusions about the future. Prompts like "what does this predict?" are off-brand and off-brief.
- **Health or diagnosis implications.** No fields related to health, energy, vitality as physical outcomes. The life line does not predict longevity; no worksheet field should imply it does.
- **Fear-based framing.** No labels like "danger signs", "warning lines", "trouble markers", or "negative formations". All variations are described neutrally.
- **Fixed certainties.** Prompts should consistently use "traditionally associated with", "often interpreted as", "may suggest" — not "means", "shows", "indicates" as assertions.
- **Relationship judgments or compatibility scores.** The marriage/relationship lines worksheet area does not belong in this pack. There is no compatibility scoring, no relationship verdict prompt.
- **Anything implying palmistry replaces professional advice.** The pack should include a single brief disclaimer on the cover or first page; it does not need to be repeated on every sheet.

## 6. Design and Brand Guidelines

### Print vs screen

The site uses a dark colour palette (deep midnight indigo background, warm cream text, gold accents) that is inappropriate for printables. Worksheets should use a light, print-ready palette. This is not an inconsistency — it is a different medium with different constraints.

**Recommended print palette:**
- Background: white or very light warm cream (#faf8f4 equivalent)
- Text: near-black (#1a1a1a or similar)
- Accent / headers: dark warm brown or deep indigo — something that reads as premium without requiring colour ink. The pack should be fully usable printed in greyscale.
- Rule lines and dividers: light grey (#d0ccc4 equivalent)

### Typography

- **Headers and worksheet titles:** Cinzel (Google Fonts) — consistent with the site. If embedding fonts in the PDF is impractical via the design tool, use a similar classical serif (Cormorant Garamond, EB Garamond) as a substitute.
- **Body text and field labels:** Lora (Google Fonts) — matches the site's body font. Substitute: any legible serif at 10–12pt for print.
- **Field input lines:** Simple ruled lines, not boxes. Boxes feel like a form; lines feel like a journal. The distinction matters for tone.

### Visual style

Think sketchbook or field journal — clean, minimal, atmospheric. The worksheets should feel like something a thoughtful practitioner would carry to a session, not a corporate PDF or a brightly coloured educational handout.

- Generous white space
- Minimal use of decorative elements — if any, a single fine rule or a small circular motif consistent with the site's aesthetic
- Hand diagrams: simple, accurate line art. Not stylised, not decorative. They are reference tools.
- No photographs, gradients, or colour fills that will not print cleanly in greyscale

### Cover page

The bundle PDF should have a cover page:
- Title: "The Palmistry Path Worksheet Pack"
- Subtitle: "Seven practice sheets for observation, study, and reading"
- Tagline: "Read the lines. Discover the path."
- Brief disclaimer: "For education and personal reflection. Not a substitute for professional medical, legal, or financial advice."
- URL: palmistrypath.com

## 8. Email Sequence Ideas

This is a sketch, not a final copy brief. Each email should be drafted in the site's established voice: grounded, curious, not salesy. No urgency manufactured via countdown timers or scarcity claims unless genuinely true.

### Email 1 — Announcement

**Subject line direction:** "Something I've been building for you"

Content direction: Personal, low-key. "I've been thinking about the gap between reading about palmistry and actually sitting with someone's hand..." Introduce the idea of the worksheet pack without pitching it yet. Plant the idea, invite curiosity. End with: "More on this soon."

### Email 2 — Education

**Subject line direction:** "Why observation beats memorisation"

Content direction: Draw on the printable-palmistry-worksheets hub article. The argument: most palmistry learning stops at "what does X mean?" — the harder and more valuable skill is "can you actually find X on a hand?" Worksheets are practice for the harder skill. This email works whether or not the reader buys — it builds trust and is useful on its own.

### Email 3 — Preview

**Subject line direction:** "Here's one of the sheets (free)"

Content direction: Share Worksheet 1 — the Hand Observation Sheet — as a free download in this email. Note that it is part of the Starter Guide for new subscribers, and that the full pack includes six more. This email does the work of showing, not telling. The free sheet demonstrates the quality and approach of the whole product.

### Email 4 — Launch

**Subject line direction:** "The Worksheet Pack is ready"

Content direction: Direct and clear. What is in it (all 7 worksheets), what it costs ($7), where to get it (purchase link). Brief framing: "This is for learners who want to practice palmistry, not just read about it." One or two sentences on what each worksheet covers, then a clean CTA. No padding.

### Email 5 — Last Chance / Reflection

**Subject line direction:** "Before I move on — a thought on practice"

Content direction: Not a hard close. Acknowledge that the launch window is ending. Reiterate the core value proposition in one sentence. Then — because the site's tone is educational, not sales-driven — add something genuinely useful: a short reflection on what makes palmistry practice different from palmistry theory. End with the purchase link, clearly but not insistently. This email should feel like it would be worth sending even if it generated zero sales.

### Design and production

Design the worksheets in Canva, Adobe Express, or a comparable tool. Export as PDF. Canva is the most practical choice for version 1: it handles A4/Letter export cleanly, supports Google Fonts (Cinzel and Lora are available), and produces print-ready PDFs without requiring design software expertise.

When designing, work worksheet by worksheet against the field specifications in Section 3. The spec is written to be used as a design brief directly.

### Hosting and distribution

Options in ascending order of complexity:

- **Gumroad** — simplest. Upload the PDF, set the price, get a product link. No self-hosting required. Takes a transaction fee but requires zero code.
- **Lemon Squeezy** — similar to Gumroad, marginally better for EU VAT compliance if international sales are expected.
- **Self-hosted** — embed Stripe or similar, serve the PDF from Cloudflare R2 or similar. More control, more maintenance. Not worth it at launch volume.

Recommend Gumroad for version 1.

### Wiring up purchase links on the site

When ready to sell:
- Add a product link from `/guide/` for the free worksheets
- Add a product link from `/blog/beginner/printable-palmistry-worksheets/` for the full pack
- Consider a site-wide "Resources" nav item (not yet in the nav) once there is more than one product to list

Do not create a store page or pricing page until there is a second product. One product does not need its own page; it can be linked from the articles that support it.
