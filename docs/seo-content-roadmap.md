## 1. SEO Positioning

Palmistry Path competes on **structured education**, not on fortune-telling promises. The core differentiator is honest framing: grounded in named traditions, acknowledging cross-tradition disagreement, and never claiming predictive power.

**How to win:**
- Long-tail beginner queries ("what does a short life line mean", "which hand do you read in palmistry") where competitors over-promise
- Topic depth: most palmistry sites have one thin paragraph per line; Palmistry Path publishes full, sourced articles
- Trust signals: consistent sourcing (Cheiro, Benham, Gettings), no prediction claims, educational tone
- Structured learning path — few palmistry sites are built as curricula

**What to avoid:**
- Competing on high-volume fortune-telling queries ("palm reading free", "what is my future") — wrong intent match and contradicts site positioning
- Over-optimising any single page; build topic authority through clusters

|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage | `/` | Brand entry point, orientation | Branded searches; "learn palmistry" head term |

| Learn lesson pages | `/learn/[module]/[lesson]/` | Curriculum delivery | Curriculum/progression queries; internal links from blog |
| Blog posts | `/blog/[slug]/` | Search entry points | Informational head- and long-tail queries |
| Glossary | `/glossary/` | Term definitions | Definition queries; internal-link support |
| Guide page | `/guide/` | Email conversion | "palmistry guide", "palm reading for beginners" |
| Future paid resources | TBD | Monetisation | Transactional and mid-funnel queries |

**Role rules:**
- **Blog = search entry points.** Blog articles serve informational intent ("how to read a heart line", "what is the simian line"). They are front doors from search engines.
- **Learn = curriculum and progression.** Lessons go deeper than blog posts, assume the reader is working through a sequence, and link forward and backward within the module. They are not optimised for cold search traffic in isolation.
- **Glossary = definitions only.** Entries should be 1–3 sentences maximum, linked to the relevant lesson or article. Never expand a glossary entry into a full article — create a blog post instead and link from the glossary.
- **Guide = email conversion.** The Starter Guide page exists to collect emails. Its content is a taster, not a complete treatment of any topic.
- **Future paid resources** — begin with worksheets and a beginner workbook. Gate them only after organic traffic justifies it.

## 4. Cannibalization Rules

### Core principle
Each page type has a distinct role. When two pages could cover the same topic, role — not timing — determines which page should rank.

### Rules

**Blog vs. Learn on the same topic:**
Blog should target the cold-search user who found the site via Google. Learn lessons serve the engaged reader progressing through a curriculum. They may share a topic but must serve different depths and intents. Blog articles are complete standalone reads. Lessons can assume the reader has worked through prerequisites.

*Rule: Blog wins cold search. Learn wins depth and curriculum progression. Both can exist. Internal links should flow: blog → lesson (Go Deeper CTA) and lesson → blog (related reading).*

**Blog vs. Blog on the same topic:**
Never publish two blog posts with the same primary keyword. If a new article would compete with an existing one (e.g., a second "heart line meaning" post), either: (a) redirect the new concept into a section of the existing article, or (b) create a clearly differentiated companion post (e.g., "Forked Heart Line Meaning" is a companion to "Heart Line Meaning" — different query, different intent).

**Glossary vs. Blog:**
Glossary entries must stay short — definitions, 1–3 sentences, a link. If a glossary entry is growing into a full explanation, stop, create a blog article, and replace the glossary entry with a one-sentence definition plus a "Read more" link. A glossary entry that ranks for a head-term query is a cannibalisation risk against the more valuable blog article.

**"No fate line" and similar absence/variation queries:**
These are distinct enough from the main line articles to warrant their own posts. A searcher asking "what does no fate line mean" has a different intent than "fate line meaning." Rule: if a variation (absent, broken, forked, chained, doubled, island) generates its own distinct search query, it earns its own article. Link from the main line article to the variation article.

**Active/passive hand blog post (missing):**
The foundations lesson 04 covers this. A blog post targeting "which hand do you read in palmistry" or "left or right hand palmistry" would not cannibalize anything that currently exists — it would be a high-traffic entry point with a clear Go Deeper CTA to the foundations lesson.

- Content to pair it with: the printable worksheets article and the palm reading chart article

**2. Beginner workbook** *(medium-term — after 500+ monthly sessions)*
- A structured 30–50 page PDF covering foundations through major lines
- Based on the existing curriculum content; adds exercises for self-observation
- Pairs with the curriculum but goes deeper on practical application
- Price point: $12–20

**3. Book/resource recommendations** *(near-term)*
- Honest affiliate page (Amazon/Bookshop.org) built around sources already cited in articles
- Recommended list: Cheiro's *Palmistry for All*, Benham's *Laws of Scientific Hand Reading*, Fincham's *The Spellbinding Power of Palmistry*, West's *The Life in Your Hands*
- Do not add affiliate links unless you can honestly recommend each title

**4. Mini-course** *(long-term — after sustained traffic)*
- A guided version of the foundations + lines modules
- Video or structured written format
- Do not build before the content earns it — 1,000+ monthly sessions minimum before this is worth the production cost

**5. Practice readings/examples library** *(long-term)*
- Annotated example palms with traditional commentary
- Requires careful framing: "here is how this configuration is traditionally interpreted" not "this person is..."
- Could be a gated resource in a future membership

### What not to monetise

- **Deterministic prediction content** — "your fate line says you will..." — never, at any price point
- **Health and longevity claims** — the short life line myth, the mercury line as health indicator — frame as tradition, not medicine
- **An expensive course before the audience exists** — validate with free content and a workbook first
- **Spammy affiliate pages** — one honest, sourced recommendation page is better than a dozen thin "best palmistry tools" posts
- **Paid access to educational content that should be free** — the curriculum lessons and blog articles remain free; gate only premium resources (PDFs, course supplements)
